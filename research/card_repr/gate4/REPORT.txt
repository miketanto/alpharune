Gate 4 (TESTPLAN.md): does a TRAINED self-play network understand card mechanics IN CONTEXT? Rengar mirror, 2026-11-19
=====================================================================================================================

ANSWER
------
No. Neither network passes gate 4 in any (mechanic, context) cell, B2 or id.
The value head knows who is winning: its root value correlates r = 0.67 with the playout win rate over 431 positions.
But it does not track the ground-truth effect of a single counterfactual edit. Across positions, in every cell, the
correlation between the model's dvalue and the playout dwin has a 95% bootstrap CI that includes 0.
Pooled over all 855 non-null edits there is a weak positive correlation:
- B2 r = +0.15 [+0.07, +0.24];
- id r = +0.14 [+0.06, +0.22].
That comes mostly from differences BETWEEN cells (e.g. both nets lower the value when an opposing hidden card is
present), not from tracking within a cell.

What the networks get, and what they don't (B2 / id; truth = playout dwin, model = dvalue in win units):
- Might +-2 on a key unit: truth -0.14 / +0.16 / -0.12, the largest effects measured. The value head barely moves:
  |dv| about 0.01 for both nets, about 10x too small, with r about 0. FAIL for both.
- [Assault 2] given this turn to a unit that can attack now: truth +0.062 +- 0.027, and the model has the WRONG SIGN.
  B2 dv = -0.081, id dv = -0.087; sign agreement 0 of 12 clear positions for both. This is the clearest result of the
  gate: the effective-Assault token field (the only thing this edit changes in the observation) is read, but read
  backwards. Plausibly a training correlation: almost the only unit with effective Assault > 0 is Inferna, a
  1-Might unit.
- Context, Assault on an attacker vs on a defender (it expires before the defender can attack):
  - truth: +0.062 vs +0.003, so the rule is visible in the ground truth;
  - B2: -0.081 vs +0.008;
  - id: -0.087 vs -0.025.
  Neither net shows the right context dependence. B2 at least treats the defender case as near-null.
- [Shield 2] on an enemy defender: truth -0.086 +- 0.049.
  - B2: right sign but tiny (dv -0.007, sign agreement 0.86 of 14 clear positions).
  - id: larger (-0.044, 1.00 of 14), but id gives about the same response to Shield on its own ATTACKER (-0.065,
    truth +0.009) and on its own exhausted unit in base (null, -0.046).
  So id reads "a Shield field anywhere = bad", not the defending context.
- Hidden card present vs absent: truth is large.
  - Opponent's hidden card: -0.137 +- 0.054. Both nets lower the value (B2 -0.076, id -0.085; sign agreement 0.82 /
    0.79 of 34 clear), but with r about 0 across positions: they register THAT the card is there, not how much it
    matters.
  - Own hidden card: +0.073 +- 0.030. B2 is blind (+0.006). id has the right sign on average (+0.028, sign 0.93 of 15
    clear), but r = 0.01.
- Kinkou Initiate condition crossing (other units' total Might 5): truth +0.127 / -0.109, but the edit is a Might
  change on another unit, so it is confounded with Might. Both nets near 0 (|dv| 0.005-0.02). B2's true-to-false cell
  is the only "WEAK" verdict (r +0.24 [-0.09, +0.55], sign 0.58).
- Legion (Noxus Hopeful in hand), Darius's second-card condition (board / hand), Star Spring's once-per-turn stamp,
  Inferna's Assault while it cannot attack:
  - Under this policy at these positions the ground truth is about 0: +0.005, +0.025, +0.009, +0.021 (relevant) /
    -0.001 (irrelevant), +0.001.
  - Too few clear positions (2-20) to test tracking; the models' responses are small too.
  These mechanics are not shown to be understood; there is nothing to track here.
- Unit at a battlefield vs in base: truth varies in sign by position (mean -0.015, 26 of 40 clear).
  r = +0.05 (B2) / -0.05 (id). FAIL.
- Null edits:
  - B2 passes 2 of 3 (cards-played with no reader 0.007; Shield on own exhausted base unit 0.006). It fails narrowly
    on Assault for an enemy unit in base: |dv| 0.012 against a limit of 0.011.
  - id passes 1 of 3: it responds -0.028 and -0.046 to the two keyword nulls. id's keyword responses are mostly
    "any keyword field changes the value", whatever the context.

B2 vs id
- Strength: id 0.955 [0.917, 0.976] vs B2 0.905 [0.856, 0.938] against ISMCTS-100 (greedy, 200 games each). The
  CIs overlap.
- Understanding: neither passes any cell.
  - B2 is more inert (|dv| 0.021 pooled vs 0.030) and has the cleaner nulls.
  - id reacts more to keyword fields, but context-blind.
  - Both share the inverted Assault reading.
- On a secondary ground truth (the id net's OWN policy playing out 72 of the positions; tables_idpolicy.txt), the
  conclusions are the same: no cell passes, pooled r +0.09 [-0.05, +0.23] for id.

Rare contexts (gate 3 D3)
- "Inferna can attack now" occurs in 6 of about 2,500 collected main-phase positions (0.2%); 3 were played out.
  TOO-FEW: the attacking context of Inferna's own Assault cannot be tested from the trained policy's games.
  (An early checkpoint had 12 of 155: the trained policy seldom leaves Inferna ready to attack.)
- Defending contexts were reachable as edits: "defender" = an enemy unit at a battlefield a legal move would attack,
  40 positions each for Assault / Shield. But the units ACTING while defending (D3) are not what gate 4 measures:
  every edit is judged at the turn player's main-phase decision. The only decision the engine can snapshot and
  resume is the main-phase one; see caveats.
- Own hidden card (27 positions), Kinkou true-to-false (29) and Darius on board (50) were reached only with a second,
  rare-cell collection of 600 more games.

Verdict rules (analyze.py):
- PASS: bootstrap r CI above 0, and sign agreement >= 0.7 on >= 4 clear positions out of >= 8.
- WEAK: r > 0.2 and sign agreement > 0.5.
- TOO-FEW: fewer clear positions than that.
- Null cell NULL-OK: mean |dv| <= half the pooled mechanic |dv| of that net, and |mean dv| <= 0.01.

Ground-truth precision: N = 384 playouts per variant.
- Original-position win rate 95% CI: median +-0.046 (max +-0.050).
- Per-edit paired effect CI: median +-0.060.

- Resource incident:

  - GPU: 11.7 of 12.3 GB in use, mostly our two processes; WDDM oversubscription. At 16:43 the GPU had held 772 MiB

PHASE B: HARNESS (gate4/)
-------------------------
- rbenv extension (research code only; nothing under src/ edited; no commits)
  - Source: gate4/rbenv_gate4_ext.cpp, appended to selfplay/rbenv/rbenv.cpp. Backup of the previous file:
    gate4/rbenv.pre_gate4.cpp.bak.
  - Other rbenv.cpp changes: Env::hist records every applied choice index (applyTracked), and Env::tok_obj the token's
    object id. Result carries the playout job. startGame() hands off to a playout start when the handle is a playout
    handle.
  - Normal rbenv behaviour is unchanged. Re-run on the final build: test_determinism ok (410 steps, 24 games
    identical); test_fields OK (all v2 / v4 checks 0 mismatches, 300 games).
  - C API:
    - rbenv_hist, rbenv_resumable, rbenv_tok_obj;
    - rbenv_po_create (replay a position), rbenv_po_describe;
    - rbenv_po_add_variant (edit + resume + Cleanup + root observation), rbenv_po_variant_info, rbenv_po_root_obs;
    - rbenv_po_start / rbenv_po_busy / rbenv_po_results (playout jobs, played with rbenv_observe / rbenv_step).
  - Python: gate4/po.py.
- Snapshot: a position = (game seed, history of applied choice indices).
  - Replay is deterministic: an earlier test replayed and played the same seeds twice with identical results.
  - Only a top-level main-phase decision can be snapshotted: GameEngine::isResumableDecision; resumeFromSnapshot
    accepts nothing else.
- Edits go through the engine's own path:
  - StateEditor::moveObject / moveObjectToBattlefield for zone changes;
  - otherwise the fields the engine's own effects write: giveTemporaryKeyword's "this turn" Assault / Shield, base
    Might, cards_played_this_turn, Star Spring's __starspring_turn_p<i> stamp.
  - "Remove Assault" sets the value to 0 for this turn with the matching negative temp value, so the expiration step
    restores it.
  - A probe engine resumes the edited state (resumeFromSnapshot: the Main Phase settles the chain and staged combats
    and runs Cleanup, recomputing auras, Might, deaths and control). The edit is kept only if the engine then asks
    the same player a main-phase decision. That post-Cleanup state and decision are the variant's root.
  - Result: 0 of 975 edits refused, and every edit still visible in the post-Cleanup root state (check_edits.txt).
- Playouts:
  - Each (variant, seed) resumes the root with the engine RNG seeded from the seed, and both Main Decks reshuffled
    (the order is hidden from both players; hands and board are the true ones).
  - The network plays both seats.
  - All variants of a position use the same seeds (common random numbers): the effect is a paired difference.
  - A 64-playout screen skips positions whose original win rate is outside [0.08, 0.92] (212 skipped): decided
    positions carry no signal.
- Positions:
  - Taken from the final B2 net's own SAMPLED self-play: 300 games (positions.json, 871 candidates), plus 600 games
    for the rare cells only (positions2_rare.json).
  - Main-phase decisions from turn 2 on, p = 0.12, at most 3 per game.
  - The edit plan comes from the engine's description plus the root observation:
    - attacker = a turn-player unit some legal move would send into a combat as attacker (act[47] = 1);
    - defender = an enemy unit at such a destination.
  - Caps: 40 positions per cell (10 per shard), and up to 80 more for the rare cells.
- Ground-truth policy: SAMPLED, not greedy.
  - A greedy pilot (8 positions, 49 edits, N = 128, gate4/tmp/res_test.jsonl) gave original win rates of 0.99, 0.00,
    0.13, 0.75, 0.00, 1.00, 0.02 and 1.00.
  - In it, 46 of 49 edits had ZERO discordant seeds: greedy self-play from a position is nearly deterministic even
    with reshuffled decks, so it cannot measure an edit.
  - The same positions sampled gave 5-50% discordant seeds (tmp/res_samp.jsonl).
  - The value head is trained on the sampling policy's returns, so the sampled playout win rate is exactly what it
    estimates.
  - Strength evaluations (phase A) stay greedy.
- Model response, at the root decision of each variant:
  - dv = (v(edited) - v(original)) / 2, the decider's perspective;
  - policy TV between the root distributions, options matched by the engine's option text; an option present on
    one side only counts in full.
  - id's responses were added after the fact by rebuilding each variant (add_model.py; the rebuilt legal lists were
    asserted equal).
- Compute:
  - Playouts first ran on CPU (no GPU while a training ran), then on the GPU, 4 processes capped at 0.2 of its memory
    each. About 0.7M games in total.
  - Windows had 5-6 GB free RAM after the coordinator's intervention; WSL used up to 5.6 GB.

CAVEATS
-------
1. One ground truth for both nets: the B2 policy's playouts. A value head estimates its OWN policy's returns.
   - The id net was also checked against its own policy on 72 positions (results_idpol_s2.jsonl,
     tables_idpolicy.txt), with the same conclusion. That subset is small (10 per cell).
2. Only main-phase roots. The engine resumes only top-level main-phase snapshots, so:
   - no edit is judged at a combat, showdown, chain or target decision;
   - "attacking vs defending" is a context of the edited UNIT (can attack now / is an enemy defender), not of the
     decision;
   - the D3 contexts (units acting while defending) stay untested. Testing them needs a snapshot at other decisions,
     e.g. replay to the decision, then edit through mutableState() while parked, which the engine supports for
     ChainPriority / ShowdownFocus.
3. Some cells have ~0 ground truth under this policy at these positions:
   - Legion, Darius (hand), Star Spring, Inferna's Assault while it cannot attack.
   - "Fails to track" there is untestable, not a demonstrated failure. The positions were not chosen for the
     mechanic being decisive.
4. Confounds:
   - The Kinkou edit is a Might change on another unit, so it carries the Might effect too.
   - The Legion / Darius / null "played" edit changes cards_played_this_turn, which also changes the global feature
     g69.
   - "location: bf -> base" can also change who holds the battlefield.
5. The "Assault wrong sign" result rests on 40 positions (12 clear), consistent for both nets. The edit changes only
   tok[22] (effective Assault) and keyword bits the encoder doesn't read: Might is unchanged until the unit attacks.
   So the response is the net's reading of that field. A follow-up could check the training-data correlation of
   tok[22] with the return.
6. Training is short: 150 updates (about 29k games). Gate 3's wiring tests passed, but the fields here are clearly
   under-used. The gate-3 note that the Star Spring stamps and CombatDamage Might paths are about 10x weaker than
   other fields is consistent with this.
7. Per-position precision is +-0.06 for an edit, so "clear" positions are those with |dwin| >~ 0.12. Small real
   effects (Legion's cost, Star Spring) are below what one position can resolve. The cell means have CIs of +-0.01 to
   +-0.05.
