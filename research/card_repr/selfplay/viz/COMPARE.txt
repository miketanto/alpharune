Seed 7001: the old network's blunders, and what the new one did
---------------------------------------------------------------
Both games have the same deal: Star Spring (P1) and Emperor's Dais (P2); P1's hand is Punch First, Nidalee,
First Mate and Noxus Hopeful. The old network mulliganed Noxus Hopeful (a 7.2% sample; keep was 15.6%).
The new one keeps (89.5%). Turns 0-3 are almost the same: Rengar takes Star Spring by killing Pakaa Cub,
Kinkou Initiate takes the Dais (2-1), and on turn 4 ISMCTS kills Rengar with Inferna + Pit Rookie (2-2).

1. "Pit Rookie to base" (old turn 7, a 2.4% sample; Dais was 91%).
   NEW turn 7, same spot (4-3 up, holding the Dais): Pit Rookie -> Emperor's Dais (45%, its top choice), then
   Nidalee -> Dais and First Mate -> Dais. Each Pridestalker / First Mate buff went to the Irresistible Faefolk
   (permanent +1, up to 3 Might). It ended the turn with SIX units (18 Might) on the Dais.
   Turn 11 (6-5 up) is a similar choice. It played Pit Rookie to base at 52.5% (Dais 39.4%), then pulled both
   Faefolk back to base. Those three units became its turn-13 attack force, so that base play had a purpose.
2. "Two 1-Might Faefolk parked on the Dais" (old turn 7: First Mate also left for base on a 7.4% sample,
   so 2 Might held the Dais; ISMCTS's Inferna 4M killed both and conquered, 4-5).
   NEW: one Faefolk on the Dais from turn 5, and it never sat there alone. ISMCTS attacked on turn 8 with
   Pit Rookie + Rengar (10 Might) into 18 Might of defenders. It lost both attackers; P1 lost Nidalee and
   Noxus Hopeful but kept the Dais. On turn 12 Pyke attacked the Dais alone and died to First Mate + Pit
   Rookie (Pit Rookie died too). The Dais was held on turns 5, 7, 9, 11, 13 and 15, and the last hold
   was the 8th point.
3. "Attacking Star Spring one unit at a time" (old turn 9: Pit Rookie alone, "Move no more units" sampled at
   28% where adding First Mate was 72%; then First Mate alone; value +0.10 -> -0.50 -> -0.73).
   NEW turn 9 (5-4 up, Star Spring held by a P2 stack): no attack. It played a Faefolk to the Dais and
   ended the turn (End Turn 45%, Sabotage 28%).
   NEW turn 13 (7-6 up): ONE move with all three units (Faefolk, Faefolk, Pit Rookie; join picks 89.7% and
   77.1%), plus both Faefolk "move an enemy unit here" triggers (91%). That is the opposite of piecemeal.
   The attack still lost: 6 Might against First Mate 3 + Inferna 1 + Noxus 4. All three attackers died and
   only Noxus Hopeful fell. It cost the network no points, because Star Spring was P2's anyway and the
   Dais (plus a hidden Pakaa Cub played after the combat) held for the winning point on turn 15.
   (The other "P1 attack" in the turn list, turn 12, was not the network's choice: ISMCTS's Faefolk moved
   Kinkou Initiate into Star Spring, where it died.)
Value: the old network fell from +0.35 (turn 7) to -0.73 after its turn-9 attacks. The new one sat at
+0.2..+0.8 and followed the trades: -0.01 after the turn-8 combat, +0.79 at 6-5, and -0.47 on turn 14 at 7-7
after ISMCTS played Darius. That last one was too pessimistic: the Dais was still its own, and holding it
gave the win at the start of turn 15.

General patterns (all six games; numbers from the transcripts)
---------------------------------------------------------------
* A sharper policy, and the top choice is what it plays. The chosen option's mean probability per game was
  46-57% (old) and 66-76% (new). Choices under 15%: old 4-13 per game, all of them samples; new 0 in five
  games, 1 in 7001 (a damage assignment among many near-equal splits).
* Tighter combat. The old network attacked alone into stronger or equal defenders in 7001 (turn 9, twice;
  turn 11) and in 7002 (turn 13: 5 Might into 10, then 4 into 6). The new network's attacks are mostly
  coordinated multi-unit moves into weak defence: 7002 t5 (2 units, 11 Might vs 2), 7102 t13 (4 units,
  15 vs 2), 7103 t10 and t14 (3 units, 12 vs 3). It also attacks less. It mostly stacks the battlefield it
  holds and lets ISMCTS attack into it: in 7003 it never attacked, and ISMCTS attacked Seat of Power six times
  into 13-23 Might of defenders. Its one costly attack was 7102 t5: Rengar (7) into Emperor's Dais against
  Grim Apothecary (3). ISMCTS answered with Pyke (5), so Rengar died. Kai'Sa then attacked alone and conquered.
  That was one attack after another, but it scored the point.
* Mulligan. Old: flat (top option usually 12-18%, every mulligan a 6-11% sample). New: decisive where it is
  obvious (keep 89.5% / 86.7% / 86.1%, "mulligan Sabotage" 93.9% in 7002). In 7003 and 7101 it was still split
  (31.9% vs 19.3%, 36.2% vs 32.1%). It learned to throw back Sabotage (a hand-disruption spell) and to keep
  creature curves.
* Value estimate. Mean squared error of v against the result, over all network decisions: 0.248 (old) and
  0.226 (new). That is the same within the noise of six games, most of which both networks won. The behaviour
  differs: the old value stayed at +0.7..+0.9 while 6-7 down in 7101. The new value falls in close games
  and comes back when it gets ahead (7001: -0.01 at 4-4 after the big trade, -0.47 at 7-7; 7003: +0.03 at
  6-6 on turn 14, then +0.82). In the lopsided games it reaches +0.9 or more by mid-game, as the old one did.
  So it is more responsive to close scores, but it can still be too pessimistic (7001 t14), and six games
  do not show calibration.
* Pace: 7101 went from an 8-7 squeaker to an 8-3 win in 11 turns. 7003 went the other way: from a 8-1 rout to a
  17-turn 8-7 holding game, because the new network did not attack at all.
